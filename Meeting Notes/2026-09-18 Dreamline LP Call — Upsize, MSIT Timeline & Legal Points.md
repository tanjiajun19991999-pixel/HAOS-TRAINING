## 6. Commercial & legal points

### 6.1 Contracting entity / GST
- GP has **no offshore entity**, so payment would go to the **IMM Singapore entity**, which attracts **9% GST**.
- GP's offer: if the LP prefers the Singapore entity, GP is fine with it — but the 9% would need to be absorbed in the management fee, given the extra cost.
- **LP: no preference, happy to pay the Singapore entity.** On a Singapore-to-Singapore transaction, the GST should be clean/recoverable.
- **Agreed:** contract via the Singapore entity. Easier for both sides, and no translation issues.
- ⚠️ Later in the call GP noted "we will be subject to the GST" — worth a line of confirmation on who ultimately bears it.

### 6.2 FX and funding
- **Agreed principle:** **fix the USD commitment amount**; the **FX rate is fixed as of the date of settlement** — i.e. the **capital call date**.
- Applies to both the **initial/principal capital commitment** and the **management fees**.
- Nothing changes on the **USD terms**; whatever that translates to in KRW is settled at the capital call.
- KRW shareholding percentage is then derived from that converted amount, and GP will confirm back.
- **Why the LP asked:** the Korean-side fund needs to know the exact KRW commitment. If FX moves unfavourably and the KRW commitment rises, the **infra fund has to top up** the gap.
- **Practical mechanism agreed:** build in a **buffer** — call slightly *under* the full USD 30m equivalent in KRW (e.g. USD ~29.5m) so an adverse FX move is absorbed. This mirrors how the LP's Korea office handles USD investments.
- **Capital-call FX hedging facilities** were raised as an option (small fee over ~5 days) — **LP will not use one** for this capital call.
