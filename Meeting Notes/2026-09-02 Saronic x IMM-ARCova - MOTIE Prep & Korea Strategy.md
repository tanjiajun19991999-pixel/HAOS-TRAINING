## 8. Attracting Korean Tier 1 / Tier 2 suppliers to the US

**Saronic's problem statement:** SHI's message has consistently been that they're open to it and agree it's valuable, but they **don't have the voltage to direct their suppliers**. So how is the Korean government thinking about the incentives?

**JJ's answer — it's commercial, not political:**

- Talking to Tier 1s (e.g. the one that works with HD and also with Samsung), it's a **commercial decision about expanding their business**.
- Precedent: **suppliers followed HD overseas** when HD expanded its shipyards. The implicit bargain: *put in capex and move to our new site, and you get the orders.* Not necessarily a formal contract, but that's the mechanism.
- **Same logic applies to Saronic:** if a supplier puts capex into a facility at Port Alpha, they expect near-guaranteed order flow from Saronic — which makes their capex stable.
- **Government role:** that **capex investment can be supported**. Open question whether it counts toward the **$150bn** trade-deal number — but JJ's view is that **if it does count, that's exactly how MOTIE will want to incentivise suppliers to come in**. Korean companies expanding into the US site, counting toward the 150, helping US shipbuilding.
- **What suppliers actually need to commit:** a clear roadmap, a visible order book, and confirmation that **Samsung and others are clearly part of it**. Realistically it's **probably too soon** for firm commitments today, but JJ expects it to come together.

> [!tip] Saronic's near-term idea
> Bringing down **material costs** meaningfully is critical to starting the flywheel in certain customer segments. Possible bridge: **buy materials from Korea initially**, which sets the conditions for suppliers to make the capex investment in US production later. JJ agreed this fits the pattern.
