### Submarine cables

| # | Question | Answer | Theme | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPC-15 | What is the contractual nature of the cables and the competitive dynamic? | Demand is driven by APAC cross-border traffic growth (~40–50% CAGR over the last 5 years, largely pre-AI monetisation). IMM underwrites ~40% (seller ~50%, KKR 50–60%) over 5–7 years. Supply in Korea is structurally tight: 7–8 of ~10 operational cables were built around 2000 against a 25–30 year useful life, and 2–3 are expected to be decommissioned within 2–3 years — coinciding with the new build pipeline. Market studies: Bain (Dreamline), A.T. Kearney (Spectrum). | Market | Answered |
| SPC-16 | Who is the operational cable contracted to? | SJC2, operational since ~Q3 2025; largest customer is Microsoft. | Contracts | Answered |
| SPC-17 | How does cable revenue actually work? | Two contract types: IRU (indefeasible right of use) and leasing/capacity contracts. Hyperscalers prefer IRU — a defined capacity carved out for exclusive use — paid via a large upfront payment plus an ongoing O&M fee of 4% of contract value. Terms are typically 10–15 years, trending to 15 years plus a 10-year extension. | Revenue model | Answered |
| SPC-18 | Is there margin in the 4% O&M fee? | Yes. The 4% is calculated on contract value, whereas O&M cost is a function of construction cost — so once capacity is substantially sold, a single full-capacity IRU covers the operating cost base. | Economics | Answered |
| SPC-19 | Ten consortium members on the operational cable — why are they all there? | Aside from Meta, the members are the incumbent telecommunications operator in each country; they join a consortium rather than build alone. | Consortium | Answered |
| SPC-20 | How do you get into a consortium if you are not a telco? | You need a Korean telecommunications licence (held by SK Broadband and by Dreamline), which confers cable landing service rights. Entry then varies by project: for Dreamline, Microsoft and AWS approached directly; on the SK side, the group has acted as a lead consortium member. Once known as an active buyer, inbound invitations follow. | Access | Answered |
| SPC-21 | Does the cable under construction have IRUs signed yet? | No. Binding consortium contracts and the EPC contract are in place, but commercial onboarding of customers typically starts ~1 year before the commencement date, with marketing running throughout. | Contracting | Answered |
| SPC-22 | Isn't building without contracted capacity speculative for a financial investor? | Risk sits pre-signing, not post-signing: before binding consortium contracts, counterparties are large domestic incumbents with strong bargaining power and abandonment risk is high. Once contracts are signed, terms are fixed and replacing an exiting member is straightforward given current appetite for cable assets. For Spectrum specifically, capacity serves both group and external demand: intra-group sales to SK companies at arm's length, with two-thirds to three-quarters targeted for external sale. | Risk | Answered |
| SPC-23 | Is Spectrum's cable the same one Dreamline invested in? | No — different cables. | Clarification | Answered |
