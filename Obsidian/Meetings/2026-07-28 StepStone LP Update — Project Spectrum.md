## 5. Submarine cable — commercial thesis

**Demand:**
- APAC cross-border data traffic has grown at **~40–50% CAGR over the last 5 years**, largely *before* AI monetisation.
- Growth assumptions: **seller ~50%**; **IMM ~40% (deliberately more conservative)**; **KKR 50–60%** over the next 5–7 years.
- Market studies: **Bain** (Dreamline) and **A.T. Kearney** (Spectrum); Dreamline also has sector advisers on its board. Expert views on direction and magnitude are broadly consistent.

**Supply:**
- Korea is structurally under-supplied — most cables run Singapore↔Japan as the two regional hubs.
- Of ~10 operational cables touching Korea, **7–8 were built around 2000** → ~25 years old against a **25–30 year useful life**.
- Market soundings indicate **2–3 of the 8 legacy cables will be decommissioned in the next 2–3 years**, timed with the new development pipeline coming to market.

**Contract mechanics:**
- Two contract types: **IRU** (indefeasible right of use) and leasing/capacity contracts.
- Hyperscalers (Microsoft, AWS, Meta, Google) are latency- and capacity-security sensitive and prefer **IRU**: a defined capacity (e.g. 5Tb of a 10Tb cable) carved out for their exclusive use.
- Economics: **large upfront payment in year 1**, then an ongoing **O&M fee of 4% of contract value**. O&M *cost* is a function of **construction cost**, so once capacity is ~fully sold via IRU, **operating costs are covered by a single IRU contract** — margin on the 4% is real.
- Terms typically **10–15 years**, market trending to **15 years with a 10-year extension option**.

**Consortium dynamics:**
- Operational cable (**SJC2**): largest customer is **Microsoft**; ~10 consortium members including **Meta** and, otherwise, the incumbent telco in each country (they join rather than build alone).
- The cable under construction has **4 effective consortium members** (a 5th acts only as a landing party at destination); SK Broadband is one of the **lead** members. **Binding consortium contracts and EPC contract are in place.**
- **Entry requirement:** a Korean telecommunications licence — held by SK Broadband and by Dreamline — which confers the right to provide cable landing services.
- How you get in varies by project: for Dreamline, **Microsoft and AWS made direct approaches**. Once you are known as an active buyer, inbound invitations follow.
- **Risk profile shifts sharply at binding signature.** Pre-signing, consortium members are large domestic incumbents with strong bargaining power, negotiations are hard, and **project abandonment risk is high**. Post-signing, terms are fixed, and **replacing an exiting member is comparatively easy** given strong current appetite (including from non-telcos) to own cable assets.
- **Commercial onboarding of customers typically begins only ~1 year before the commencement date**, though marketing runs throughout — so the cable under construction has no IRUs signed yet.
- Capacity split on Spectrum's cables: some sold **intra-group to SK group companies at arm's length**; **two-thirds to three-quarters targeted for external sale**.
